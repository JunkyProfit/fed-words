    15 Key Findings from the Transcripts

    Concrete word-count patterns across 40 years of Federal Reserve Chairs, eight US Presidents, and S&P 500 CEOs—normalized per 1,000 words.

    Finding #03 · Fed Chairs
    Every Fed Chair said “inflation” more than the one before

    From Volcker (0.96/1k) and Greenspan (1.37) to Bernanke (2.71), Yellen (5.84), and Powell (11.53/1k).
    Finding #05 · Federal Reserve
    AI dominates Fed governors’ speeches, not the Chair’s

    Barr (6.84/1k, 300 uses) and Lisa Cook (6.42/1k, 280 uses) lead on “ai”; Powell is at 0.00.
    Finding #08 · US Presidents
    Every President from Carter to Biden has a signature word

    Carter “energy” (3.82/1k), Reagan “tax” (3.52), Clinton “people” (5.78), Obama “jobs” (2.57), Trump “great” (3.08), Biden “democracy” (1.35).
    Finding #11 · S&P 500 CEOs
    Tech CEOs top 12–14 “AI” mentions per 1,000 words

    Robbins (14.19/1k), Amon (13.88), Pichai (13.40), and Su (12.03) vs. 0.00 for Abel (Berkshire) and Ortberg (Boeing).

    From Mouth to Math

    Word counts from official speeches, testimony, letters and court opinions by Federal Reserve officials, Chief Executive Officers and United States Government Officials.

    When a document becomes available to the public, each word is ranked according to how often it’s used in that document. This provides the ability to derive the substance of a speech at a glance, as well as access to every instance where each word was used in the actual document or speech. MouthMath makes mundane words not so mundane.

    MouthMath.com was created to be politically neutral, with no bias: every speaker is counted the same way. Not all speeches are publicly available.

    All content is available on a best-effort basis and counts are updated as new material becomes available. Nothing on MouthMath.com is financial, investment or legal advice.
