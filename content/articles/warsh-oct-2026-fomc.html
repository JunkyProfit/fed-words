Published October 31, 2026 · Analysis · Federal Reserve

Kevin Warsh held his fourth press conference as Fed Chair on October 28, 2026, after the Federal Open Market Committee meeting. MouthMath counted only the parts he spoke himself, his opening statement and his answers, and left out the reporters’ questions. That came to 4,167 words. This post looks at what those words show on their own and how they compare with his three earlier press conferences in June, July and September.

Everything below is a word count. It tells you what he said most often, not what he meant or what the Fed will do next. Common words such as “the”, “and” and “we” are left out of the rankings.

The October 28 top words

In October, “policy” came first with 25 uses. “Inflation” and “going” were tied for second at 20 each, followed by “think” (19), “last” and “question” (17 each), and “economy” and “financial” (16 each). “Prices” and “colleagues” came next with 14 each.

Some of these are plain speaking habits. “Going” and “think” rank high at every press conference because answering live questions sounds like “I think” and “we’re going to”. “Question” and “last” are similar: the October session had a lot of “your question” and references to the last meeting or the last few months. The more telling words are the topic words, and two of them stand out.

Policy and inflation, tied overall

Across all six Warsh documents on MouthMath (four press conferences, his July testimony to Congress and his August speech, 23,297 words in all), “inflation” and “policy” are tied at exactly 119 uses each. No other word is close: “fed” is next with 94.

“Policy” was the steadier word. It sat between 24 and 26 uses in June, July and October and dipped only in the shorter September session (12). “Inflation” peaked in July with 28 uses. Measured per 1,000 words, October had Warsh’s highest rate yet for “policy” (6.0) while “inflation” (4.8) was about in the middle of his range.

What changed from earlier press conferences

Three changes are clear in the numbers:

  • “Data” faded. It went from 22 uses in June (4.1 per 1,000 words) to 16 in July, 9 in September and just 4 in October (1.0 per 1,000). In his first press conference, talking about the data was one of his most frequent themes. By October it was rare.
  • “Financial” doubled. He said it 8 times in June and in July and 6 times in September, then 16 times in October, his highest rate so far (3.8 per 1,000 words).
  • “Colleagues” and “productivity” rose. “Colleagues” reached 14 uses, his most at any press conference, and “productivity” reached 7, also a high, after 1 in September.

Some words from the earlier sessions also dropped back. In June, “task” (25) and “force” (20) were among his top words. In October they appeared 9 and 5 times. “Markets” fell from 21 uses in July to 4 in October.

How to read this

Word counts are a quick, neutral way to see emphasis. A tie between “inflation” and “policy” says that across his first months as Chair, Warsh talked about the price goal and about the Fed’s own tools in almost exactly equal measure. The October press conference leaned a little more toward policy, financial conditions and his colleagues on the Committee, and much less toward the data.

Counts can’t show tone, and one word can be used in different ways. Every count on MouthMath links back to each sentence where the word appears, so you can read the context yourself. Open Kevin Warsh on MouthMath to see all of his words, or read his speaker profile.
