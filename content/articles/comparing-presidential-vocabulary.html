Presidents give a handful of speeches that almost everyone hears: the inaugural address, the State of the Union and addresses to the nation in big moments. MouthMath counts about 20 of these major addresses for each of eight presidents, four Republicans and four Democrats, from Jimmy Carter to Donald Trump. Comparing the counts shows both what each president focused on and how speaking styles have changed.

“People” and “America” are nearly universal

For six of the eight presidents, “people” or “America” is the most used word, and both appear in the top five for most of them. That is not surprising: these speeches are addressed to the whole country. The exceptions say something about each era. For Jimmy Carter and George H. W. Bush, “world” comes first, reflecting the Cold War and, for Bush, the Gulf War. For Ronald Reagan, “government” leads, followed by “people”, “America” and “tax”.

Each era has its own words

Beyond the shared words, each list carries the issues of its time. Carter's top words include “energy” and “oil”, from the energy crisis of the late 1970s, along with “peace”, “soviet” and “nuclear”. Reagan's include “tax”, “budget” and “spending”. Bill Clinton's include “children”, “work” and “care”. George W. Bush's include “Iraq”, “security”, “freedom” and “terrorists”, from the years after September 11, 2001. Barack Obama's include “jobs” and “work”, from speeches during and after the 2008 recession. Joe Biden's include “know”, “get” and “make”, everyday words that reflect a conversational style.

Style: “we”, “I” and “you”

Common words can also show style. Every president says “we” more than “I”, as these speeches speak for the nation. But the balance differs. Donald Trump and Joe Biden say “you” more often than the others, roughly twice as often as Carter, which suggests a more direct, conversational way of speaking to the audience. Trump also says “great” and “thank” more often than any other president on the list.

Length and variety

The speeches differ a lot in length. Barack Obama and Donald Trump average about 4,400 to 4,500 words per counted speech, while George H. W. Bush averages about 2,250. Longer speeches naturally use more different words, so the “Different words” column partly reflects length, not just vocabulary. To compare fairly, look at how often a word appears as a share of all words, which is how the comparisons above were made.

Reading the counts fairly

These lists come from a similar set of major speeches for each president, chosen the same way for all eight, but a sample is still a sample. A word count shows what was said and how often; it does not judge whether it was right. You can open any president on MouthMath, choose which speeches to include, and tap a word to read it in the original sentence.
