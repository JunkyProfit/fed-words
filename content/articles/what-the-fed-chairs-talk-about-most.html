The Chair of the Federal Reserve is one of the most closely watched speakers in the world. Markets react to single words, so it is worth asking a plain question: which words do Fed Chairs actually use most? MouthMath counts the public speeches and testimony of six Chairs, from Paul Volcker to Kevin Warsh. Here is what the counts show, with common words like “the” and “and” left out.

Inflation leads the recent Chairs

For Jerome Powell, “inflation” is the single most used word, appearing 260 times in about 22,500 words, more than once per hundred words. His documents run from August 2024 to March 2026, a period when bringing inflation back down was the Fed's main task. “Labor”, “employment” and “risks” also rank high, which fits the Fed's two goals of stable prices and maximum employment. Kevin Warsh's first two documents also put “inflation” near the top, second only to “fed”.

Janet Yellen's list, drawn from 2016 and 2017, is close behind: “rate” and “inflation” appear about 285 times each. Her list also includes “women”, which ranks in her top 15, reflecting speeches on women's role in the economy.

Crisis words for Bernanke

Ben Bernanke's most used word is “financial”, at 337 uses. His documents come from 2013 and early 2014, the years after the 2008 financial crisis, and words like “banks” and “term” rank high. As a share of all his words, “financial” appears more than four times as often for Bernanke as for Powell.

Oil and prices for Greenspan

Alan Greenspan's documents are from 2005, his last full year as Chair. Two of his top words, “prices” and “oil”, appear about 200 times each, reflecting the sharp rise in energy prices that year. “Home” also makes his top 15, from speeches about the housing market and home lending.

Growth and the world for Volcker

Paul Volcker's documents come from 1986 and 1987, after the high inflation of the late 1970s had been brought down. His list is led by “growth”, “countries”, “bank” and “world”, with a lot of attention to international debt and the global banking system. “Inflation”, which dominates Powell's list, appears about one-tenth as often for Volcker as a share of his words.

What stays the same

Some words appear near the top for nearly every Chair: “policy”, “economic”, “market” and “rates”. Those are the basic tools and subjects of the job. What changes from Chair to Chair is the problem of the day, whether it was international debt, oil prices, a financial crisis or inflation.

Keep in mind that these counts cover a sample of documents, not every word a Chair ever said, and longer documents produce bigger counts. You can open any Chair on MouthMath, choose which documents to include and tap a word to read it in context.
