Measured per 1,000 total words, “inflation” rises in every consecutive Chair’s corpus: Paul Volcker at 0.96 (70 uses in 1986–87), Alan Greenspan at 1.37 (60 uses in 2005), Ben Bernanke at 2.71 (131 uses in 2013–14), Janet Yellen at 5.84 (282 uses in 2016–17), and Jerome Powell at 11.53 (260 uses in 2024–26).

Methodology & How Words Are Counted

Tokenization: MouthMath lowercases all text, converts curly apostrophes to straight apostrophes, strips trailing possessive ’s (so Fed’s is counted with fed, while contractions like it’s stay intact), preserves u.s. as a single token, and excludes standalone numbers by default.

Normalization: Because speakers have different total word counts (from single 1,700-word shareholder letters to 90,000-word presidential speech collections), cross-speaker comparisons above are reported as mentions per 1,000 total words alongside raw occurrence counts. Singular and plural forms (such as tariff and tariffs, or customer and customers) are counted as distinct word tokens unless noted as combined.

Verify in context: Every finding above can be inspected directly in the MouthMath interactive explorer—select any speaker, choose All documents in the timeframe bar, and tap any word to read the sentences behind the count.
