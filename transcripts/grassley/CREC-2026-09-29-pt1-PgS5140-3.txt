Mr. President, now another short comment I want to make on another subject about cancer screening. Last week, I introduced a bipartisan cancer screening bill titled the “Standardizing Cancer Recommendations for Early Evaluation and Navigation of Screening Act”; SCREENS, as an acronym for that legislation and that long name of the legislation.

Cancer is the second leading cause of death in the United States. We know that access to cancer screenings increases the chance of early treatment and reduces mortality. This is especially important for rural Americans and with the rising rates of cancer in young people.

To ensure patients have access to the best information at their fingertips or in the doctor's office, the Federal Government, through the Office of National Coordinator for Health Information Technology, can help ensure cancer screening recommendations are in a data-friendly format for apps, for tools, and for interoperable technology development.

Cancer screening guidelines still sit in lengthy documents that are not easy to navigate for patients or even for providers. These guidelines are routinely updated and refined, but it is difficult to track every change.

Patients want to know: Are they at risk for cancer and when should they get screened? Patients expect doctors to have the latest information, and they often have sticky notes on the recommendations taped to their computer, but it might not be the latest information or applicable to a particular patient.

There are some leading examples that are working to address this problem, including the Cancer Screen Iowa tool and Decision Precision+. My legislation will enable more technology development for patients and providers by streamlining access to cancer screening guidelines.

Making sure cancer screening recommendations are more patient- centered is common sense. Improving cancer research, access to early detection and screening, and cancer care are critical in defeating cancer.

I am proud to say that Iowa is taking this same comprehensive approach. This includes the cutting-edge research at the University of Iowa's Holden Comprehensive Cancer Center and Iowa's use of the $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Fund that was in the tax bill of last year that is investing in rural cancer care at rural hospitals and clinics across the State. I will keep fighting to prevent cancer and improve cancer care.
